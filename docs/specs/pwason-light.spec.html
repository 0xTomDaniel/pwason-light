01 · Problem statement

Random light usually looks like software pretending to be alive.

Conventional flicker effects repeat, quantize, or call attention to their algorithm. Pwason needs a perceptually calm, nonrepeating process whose timing and physical envelopes feel closer to steady rain than to a lighting preset. Compact sparse surface contacts have removed most of the miniature-snare character and now match the Redwood aggregate tone, but their instantaneous samples remain linearly filtered noise. The preferred 13–25 s portion of Farnell’s procedural rain demonstration instead combines a locally continuous spray with occasional nonlinear high-frequency cusps. An initial fourth-power implementation made those cusps set the default peak and sounded less calm; the accepted response distributes lower microcontacts beneath the Surface Response instead. Pwason does not need bubbles, more light Channels, a second clock, or a recording layer.

02 · Solution

Keep the clockwork simple. Put wet microstructure inside the contact.

The primary process remains fixed-rate homogeneous Poisson. Speed controls the total lamp Arrival rate, Drop Population independently controls the probability of fine, mixed, and large-drop-rich audio Rain Marks, and an optional Speed–Population Link moves the two controls together. A Reference Profile separately records its detected prominent-onset density and any provisional equivalent total-Arrival rate; Redwood begins at 23.1 detected onsets/s and an operator-calibrated 120 total Arrivals/s. The Generated Rain Renderer keeps one causal response per Arrival: its compact surface window drives sparse broad audio regions and a restrained Wet Microtexture formed by thresholding, nonlinear power shaping, and high-pass filtering analytically generated noise. Quiet portions overlap into spray while rare cusps retain droplet contrast. Exact Poisson overlap—not a recording, stationary bed, bubble model, sustained per-drop hiss, or second clock—still produces the complete texture.

04 · Implementation decisions

Two deep stochastic Modules; thin sensory Adapters.

The Poisson Engine owns steady timing, Channel routing, and independent Impact Position behind one small Arrival Interface. The Generated Rain Renderer separately owns audio-only Rain Mark sampling, pure waveform generation, propagation, and exact accumulation. This preserves one high testing seam and prevents drop acoustics, LED wavelength, or block-rendering details from leaking into the primary timing process.

Temporal primitive

Steady mode is homogeneous Poisson: inter-arrival gaps are sampled from an exponential distribution at fixed λ.

Logarithmic speed

The Speed slider stores a continuous base-10 exponent from 0 through 3. Equal travel spans map to 1–10, 10–100, and 100–1000 events per second; the engine receives the resulting rate, not the exponent.

Drop Population

A separate continuous primary slider moves from fine-dominant through mixed to large-drop-rich rain. Its value selects a probability distribution over audio Rain Marks; it does not change Λ, Channel routing, or LED envelopes while unlinked. “Drop Population” is canonical because one scalar controls a distribution rather than one literal drop diameter.

Speed–Population Link

A primary switch labeled “Link Speed + Drop Population” optionally keeps both sliders at the same normalized position. Speed normalizes as u = log₁₀(Λ)/3; Drop Population spans the same u ∈ [0,1]. Linking is bidirectional: moving either control updates the other, enabling the link aligns them immediately, and disabling it preserves both current values. This relationship never changes Channel Coupling and never makes λ time-varying.

High-rate scheduling

A short-interval scheduler prepares Arrivals through a 50 ms Web Audio lookahead, avoiding browser minimum-timeout distortion while preserving each Poisson timestamp. Event counting and LEDs activate on wall time. Decorative trails are capped at 30 per second to bound DOM animation and GPU work.

Total rate

Speed is the total distinct physical Arrival rate Λ for the lamp. The number of hardware Channels never multiplies the underlying rain density, and an onset detector does not redefine Speed: it observes only the subset of Arrivals that remain prominent after Rain Mark level, surface, distance, damping, and overlap.

Event marks

Each Arrival owns amplitude, attack, decay, instantaneous rate, a routing kind, an energy-normalized eight-element Channel vector, and one continuous Impact Position. Position is independent of Channel routing.

Channel Coupling

Shared Arrivals occur at CΛ. Eight private streams each occur at (1−C)Λ/8. Their superposition remains Λ; a shared Arrival still counts once.

Listening Field

The prototype models a flat circular field around a listener h = 1.5 m above the impact plane. Field Depth continuously maps its enabled amount to a 1–30 m radius and collapses positions beneath the listener when disabled. Speed remains the total Arrival rate inside the selected field; widening it changes the distribution of audible distance, not Arrival density.

Spatial distribution

Every Arrival samples r = R√U and an independent uniform azimuth. The square root distributes impacts uniformly by surface area, producing more distant than nearby events without discrete distance bands or additional Channels.

Acoustic Propagation

Source distance is d = √(r²+h²), and full Distance Loss is h/d, normalized to 1 directly beneath the listener. Azimuth is folded into pan = (2/π) asin(sin θ), then scaled by Stereo Spread so equal angular steps produce equal pan steps without binary left/right placement. Distance Air Damping applies a distance-dependent low-pass response. This pass still adds no propagation delay, reflections, occlusion, reverberation, or binaural head model.

Rain Impact Waveform

Each response is finite, deterministic for its seed, and behaviorally brief. A sub-millisecond signed Direct Contact begins near the Arrival and a compact rounded surface-contact window supplies the event-local excitation. Leaf contacts use smooth parabolic or quartic pressure-like shaping with restrained generated roughness; litter or soil contacts use a compact Gaussian-like window over analytically generated colored noise. Default variants peak inside the compact surface-contact window, with the 90th-percentile peak no later than 25 ms, have a median 90%-energy time no greater than 35 ms, and keep the 90th percentile no greater than 55 ms. Their energy envelopes must not retain the strongly right-skewed fast-attack/exponential-noise morphology being replaced. Exact zero-valued endpoints prevent buffer seams. The Generated Rain Renderer maintains 192 reusable seeded variants for bounded live cost.

Pure-synthesis Rain Mark model

The Generated Rain Renderer deterministically assigns each caller-owned Arrival an audio-only Rain Mark drawn from Drop Population. One mark coherently relates drop-size class, velocity proxy, impact level, contact duration, target surface, surface damping, broad spectral emphasis, surface-envelope variation, and secondary-contact probability. Spectral emphasis is a correlated material property rather than eight unrelated random gains. The initial target contains leaves, leaf litter or soil, and optional wood; liquid surfaces and entrained bubbles are excluded.

Generated Surface Response

The audio implementation retains eight overlapping, ERB-derived candidate regions centered at approximately 100, 477, 990, 1,877, 4,089, 6,051, 9,289, and 18,500 Hz, but one Rain Mark normally selects only one to three of them. Spectral Sparsity is an explicit switchable Acoustic Factor: zero leaves all candidate regions active and increasing it reduces per-mark occupancy. Selection probabilities and energy normalization preserve the aggregate Redwood contours across the response population without making one drop broadband by construction. Each selected region receives generated excitation only inside the compact surface-contact window; afterward its filter evolves at zero input. Leaf responses remain broadly papery across a population; litter remains darker but retains upper detail; optional wood remains firmer but non-tonal. Low, Mid, and High Texture scale candidate groups, and Band Independence varies their generated roughness and onset detail without mapping them to LED Channels. A synthesized 20 kHz output bandwidth limit remains. No recording, recorded grain, impulse response, extracted texture waveform, stable note, stationary background bed, sustained per-drop noise feed, or hidden modal tone contributes to generated audio.

Wet Microtexture

Wet Microtexture is an explicit switchable Acoustic Factor inside the Rain Impact Waveform Implementation. While the finite surface-contact window is open, generated noise is smoothed into a correlated control signal, positive excursions above a moderate threshold are rectified and quadratically shaped, and a high-pass stage followed by a short seed-varied band-pass wavelet converts them into signed pressure-like microtransients. The default must keep the median Rain Impact crest below 1.35 times the bypass population so Wet Microtexture remains cushioned by the Surface Response; higher slider values may deliberately expose sharper cusps. Its timing is deterministic from the parent Rain Mark seed, ends with that response, and does not create Micro-splashes, additional Arrivals, a hidden event scheduler, a stationary noise bed, or a liquid/bubble interpretation.

Poisson Shot Synthesis

Generated audio is x(t) = Σ h(t−tᵢ;Mᵢ): the exact sample-wise superposition of Rain Impact Waveforms at caller-owned Steady Poisson timestamps. Sparse rates expose individual responses; dense rates become noise-like through overlap. There is no second clock, generic ambient-noise layer, granular playback, or Cox modulation.

Generated Rain Renderer

One deep Module is the sole live/offline seam for generated rain. It maps caller-owned Arrivals and the selected Drop Population to Rain Marks, waveforms, gain, continuous spatial presentation, distance filtering, and output samples while never creating or moving a Poisson timestamp. The AudioWorklet is a thin accumulation Adapter over prepared renderer plans, not a competing synthesis path.

Exact block accumulation

Generated impacts accumulate in continuous Web Audio render blocks instead of constructing thousands of short-lived nodes. This is an exact computational partition of the same Poisson sum, not a distant-field approximation: response and filter state cross block boundaries, event IDs never reseed per block, no block is looped or normalized, and no Arrival is capped or dropped. Tests prove that changing block size produces bit-identical offline output.

Micro-splash semantics

A Micro-splash is part of its parent Rain Impact Waveform, not an Arrival. It is disabled in the default single-drop baseline so the primary contact can be judged without secondary taps. Enabling it changes only the within-event response while Λ and every Poisson timestamp remain unchanged.

Acoustic Factors Interface

Twenty-one named factors are the complete adjustable generated-audio surface: Impact Body, Impact Softness, Tail Length, Event Variation, Low/Mid/High Texture, Leaf Surface, Litter / Soil, Wood Surface, Band Independence, Micro-splashes, Splash Delay, Surface Diversity, Surface Sustain, Distance Loss, Field Depth, Stereo Spread, Distance Air Damping, Density Compensation, and Compression. Each has one native enable switch and one continuous 0–100% amount slider. Disabled contributions become zero; disabled modifiers use their neutral or shortest behavior. A debounced rebuild updates future generated waveform buffers without interrupting the Poisson engine.

Listening checkpoint

The default retains Distance Loss at 70%, Mid Texture at 20%, Distance Air Damping at 60%, and Compression disabled. The single-drop baseline additionally uses 18% Impact Body, 90% Impact Softness, 32% High Texture, Micro-splashes off, and Wood Surface off. These are listening baselines rather than physical constants; every value remains independently auditionable.

Compression policy

The next generated-audio baseline bypasses artistic dynamics compression so Rain Mark level and distance remain audible. Output protection may prevent clipping, but it must not pump, raise tails, or act as a hidden texture control. The current switchable Compression Acoustic Factor remains visible until implementation replaces that path.

Source Mix

A continuous 0–100% control defaults to generated-only and crossfades toward the selected Reference Playback. Gains follow cosine and sine of the half-quadrant: 0% is generated-only, 50% gives both sources √½ gain, and 100% is Reference-only. Short Web Audio gain ramps prevent slider zipper noise.

Reference Playback

The selected bundled recording loops only after the user presses Start, only while the process is running and audio is enabled, and only when Source Mix assigns it nonzero gain. Stop or disabling audio pauses the loop. A user-selected local Rain Reference remains analysis-only and never replaces Reference Playback.

Pitch-preserving time stretch

Reference Playback rate is Speed divided by the selected profile’s equivalent total-Arrival rate when that independent calibration exists; it must never divide total Speed by detected foreground onsets while presenting the result as a physical-density match. The HTML media Adapter explicitly enables standard pitch preservation so transport changes do not transpose or spectrally shift the field recording. Steady rain is statistically stationary, so transport changes may be hard to perceive and are not equivalent to event-separated density resynthesis. Operator listening found distortion below 0.75×; the clean comparison range remains 0.75×–4×, with visible limiting outside it.

Output level

A final gain control ranges from 25% to 250% and defaults to 160%. The live oscilloscope taps the signal after this stage.

Output oscilloscope

An AnalyserNode taps the complete post-output-stage Source Mix immediately before the audio destination. The scope reports the browser’s combined digital output, not acoustic room or eardrum pressure.

Bounded Render Loop

The visual Render Loop draws once while stopped, wakes on state changes, continues only while the process or visible tails are active, and caps drawing at 30 frames per second. The oscilloscope emits at most one plotted point per CSS pixel. Audio scheduling remains independent and preserves every Arrival timestamp.

Reference Library

The default profile is Andron827’s CC0 stereo field recording of steady rainfall on earthen ground, fallen logs, and large leaves in Redwood Shores, chosen because its source description contains no stream or flowing water. The bundled file is Freesound’s openly retrievable high-quality MP3 preview of the original 44.1 kHz, 16-bit WAV and is labeled as such. The exact 60-second Amazon light-rain WAV from Xavier et al.’s study remains selectable with its DOI, CC BY 4.0 license, original filename, byte count, and MD5.

Reference Profile calibration

Every profile stores detected prominent-onset density separately from equivalent total-Arrival rate. The detected values—23.1 onsets/s for Redwood and 15.8 onsets/s for Amazon—come from the same repeatable detector over 20 seconds after a 5-second lead-in: 256-sample RMS windows, 128-sample hops at 48 kHz, positive log-energy flux above its 85th percentile, local maxima, and a 10 ms refractory interval. They are not claims about true physical-drop counts. Operator tempo matching provisionally assigns Redwood 120 total Arrivals/s, giving a prominence fraction of about 19%; Amazon remains explicitly uncalibrated until separately matched rather than inheriting a fabricated multiplier.

Reference preparation

The browser loads the selected Reference Profile automatically and may replace only the visual comparison with a user-selected local file. Analysis itself remains silent and separate from synthesis. It considers at most the first 10 seconds, finds the largest absolute-pressure sample, and extracts a 120 ms Field Window with 5 ms of pre-roll for waveform and spectrogram inspection. The Generated Rain Renderer produces an eight-second field at the selected Speed and passes it through the identical extractor. Each window therefore contains all overlapping nearby and distant Arrivals active around its strongest contact. The browser separately measures both complete profiles for spectral metrics and comparison. No recording becomes a Poisson input.

Signal analysis

A dependency-free 512-point Hann-windowed FFT retains the established averaged power-spectrum metrics. A separate 1,024-point Hann-windowed STFT centered at 1 ms hops supplies at least 80 frames across a 120 ms Field Window. The canvas bilinearly interpolates time and frequency samples onto a log-frequency, 70 dB, perceptually uniform color image; it does not enlarge discrete FFT cells into a block heatmap. Five-millisecond overlapping RMS frames also measure crest factor, envelope coefficient of variation, the 10th-percentile background floor relative to median, and mean envelope correlation across low, mid, and high regions. A shared onset detector reports prominent generated and reference events using the documented RMS-flux threshold and refractory interval. The long steady-profile pass omits stored spectrogram frames to avoid needless memory retention. Generated and measured spectra are independently peak-normalized so the graph compares shape rather than recording level.

Redwood Acoustic Target Profile

The generated comparison uses Redwood’s provisional 120 total Arrivals/s rather than its 23.1 stored detected-onset baseline. The first ten seconds define two independently peak-normalized constraints. The broad target uses nine bands over 80–20,000 Hz with edges at 80, 160, 315, 630, 1,250, 2,500, 5,000, 10,000, 16,000, and 20,000 Hz and levels −4.8, −2.8, 0, −1, −5.7, −8.2, −4.2, −4.8, and −2.6 dB; generated distance must remain below 3 dB RMS. The fine target uses thirteen bands with edges at 80, 375, 750, 1,125, 1,688, 2,438, 3,375, 4,500, 6,000, 7,875, 10,125, 12,750, 15,750, and 19,500 Hz and levels −1.6, 0, −4.1, −7.4, −10.3, −12, −13.9, −12.1, −11.1, −7.3, −10.8, −8.1, and −3.3 dB; generated distance must remain below 2 dB RMS. Diagnostic ranges are 3–6.5 kHz centroid, 12–30% energy above 8 kHz, 0.005–0.16 spectral flatness, 8–18× crest, 0.3–0.7 envelope CV, at least 35% background floor, less than 0.8 band sync, 5.5–16 kurtosis, and 0.18–0.45 100 ms CV. Redwood’s measured approximately 9.5 kurtosis remains a direction rather than a mandate: listening evidence may select a softer field inside this range. These values constrain tonal balance without importing recording samples or requiring exact reproduction of every narrow peak.

Static comparison

Matched Field Window waveforms and log-frequency spectrograms, the complete-profile spectrum, onset calibration, and steady temporal metrics render only on initial load, Speed, Rain Reference, Drop Population or Acoustic Factor changes, or a debounced resize. They do not join the live animation loop. Both 120 ms panels use the same strongest-contact extractor; the generated window comes from the complete profile rendered at the selected Speed through the same Generated Rain Renderer used for playback. The displayed generated total rate therefore follows Speed from 1–1,000 Arrivals/s, while the Reference Profile separately retains its detected-onset and equivalent-total calibration.

05 · Testing decisions

Verify what a caller can observe—not how randomness is assembled.

The Poisson Engine Interface continues to prove the unchanged steady clock and light routing. The Generated Rain Renderer remains the highest synthesis seam: its seeded outputs must prove Drop Population behavior, pure synthesis, physically coherent response populations, live/offline agreement, and block-partition invariance without exposing internal random draws or filter topology.

Good test

A known seed and settings produce the same observable sequence after any internal refactor.

Statistical sanity

A long fixed-rate run has positive gaps and a mean inter-arrival time close to 1/λ.

Browser evidence

Inspect start/stop, sound consent, live meter updates, all eight LEDs, logarithmic decade markers and intermediate Speed values, reduced motion, and narrow layouts.

High-rate evidence

At 1000 events per second, compare the event count against elapsed wall time to prove the scheduler does not collapse to the browser’s minimum timer frequency.

Signal-path evidence

Verify that the analyser receives the complete post-output-stage Source Mix, displays silence before Start, shows both sources at the midpoint, and returns toward silence after Stop.

Rain-impact evidence

Verify through the Rain Impact Waveform Interface that complete generated responses contain positive and negative pressure-like samples, remain bounded and deterministic, and begin and end at zero. Across the complete default response bank, require the 90th-percentile peak no later than 25 ms, a median 90%-energy time no greater than 35 ms, and a 90th-percentile 90%-energy time no greater than 55 ms. The population’s median temporal-energy skew must remain below 1.5 so a fast attack cannot hide a long exponential stochastic tail. With Direct Contact and Micro-splashes bypassed, enabling Wet Microtexture must measurably increase high-passed microtransient contrast over its bypass; at the default amount its median full-response crest must remain below 1.35 times bypass so it does not replace the audible Surface Response. Prove disabling Impact Body, every texture region, Wet Microtexture, and Micro-splashes yields literal silence rather than hidden ambience, and prove leaf and litter profiles remain observably distinct without periodic resonators.

Generated-renderer evidence

Through the Generated Rain Renderer Interface, prove one Arrival produces a deterministic Rain Mark and render plan with reusable response-bank identity, gain, pan, and distance-filter values; the renderer snapshots caller settings; and offline profiles require caller-owned Arrivals. This keeps Poisson timing outside the renderer and makes live, AudioWorklet, and analytical composition share one tested seam.

Rate-calibration evidence

Prove that a Reference Profile keeps detected foreground-onset density distinct from equivalent total-Arrival rate, that Redwood compares at 120 total Arrivals/s while retaining 23.1 detected onsets/s, and that an uncalibrated profile never silently inherits Redwood’s multiplier. Apply the same prominent-onset detector to synthetic and decoded profile samples, and display which rate drives generation and pitch-preserving playback.

Drop-Population evidence

At a fixed Speed and seed, moving Drop Population must preserve every Arrival timestamp, Channel route, and Impact Position while observably shifting the generated Rain Mark distribution from fine-dominant toward large-drop-rich responses. At a fixed unlinked Drop Population, moving Speed changes event density without silently changing the selected mark distribution.

Link evidence

Prove the Speed–Population Link at both endpoints and intermediate logarithmic Speed values: enabling aligns normalized positions immediately, moving either slider updates the other bidirectionally, disabling preserves both values, and no link operation changes Channel Coupling. Browser evidence verifies accessible names and visible linked state.

Rain-Mark coherence

Through renderer output populations rather than internal draws, prove that fixed seeded Rain Marks reproduce exactly and that increasingly large-drop-rich populations shift the distributions of impact energy, contact duration, surface response, and secondary-contact incidence together. Across a seeded default population, require broad non-monotonic spectral diversity, meaningful upper-band energy, and event spectra that vary while their aggregate remains complete. Switching Spectral Sparsity off must audibly and measurably broaden individual responses without changing Poisson timing. At the complete renderer seam, isolated Leaf Surface profiles must remain within 4–9 kHz centroid and 20–60% energy above 8 kHz; isolated Litter / Soil profiles must remain within 1–4 kHz and 3–25%. These are anti-extreme guardrails, not claims that one field recording isolates either surface. No test exposes private band coefficients.

Tonal-target evidence

Render the seeded default 120 Arrivals/s field through the same Generated Rain Renderer used by live playback. Independently peak-normalize the documented nine-band broad and thirteen-band fine profiles, then require less than 3 dB and 2 dB RMS distance respectively from the measured Redwood contours. Also retain the aggregate descriptor and surface-breadth guards so a narrow numerical fit cannot erase plausible mark diversity. This constrains tone through one public Interface while keeping the recording outside synthesis.

Pure-synthesis evidence

For identical Arrivals, Drop Population, Acoustic Factors, and seed, changing or removing every Rain Reference must leave generated samples bit-identical. Generated playback must make no request for a recording asset. Static dependency inspection and the public Renderer Interface together prove that recordings, grains, impulse responses, and extracted texture waveforms cannot enter synthesis.

Block-partition null test

Render one seeded Arrival sequence through the exact reference summation and through continuous blocks of several sizes, including contacts and tails crossing every boundary. Subtract the outputs and require only floating-point accumulation noise. The test also proves persistent response and filter state, stable event seeding, no per-block normalization, no repeated blocks, and no dropped Arrivals.

Spatial evidence

Across a long seeded run, all Impact Positions remain inside the configured Listening Field and the mean squared radius approaches half the field’s squared radius, as required by uniform area sampling.

Propagation evidence

Use a known 3–4–5 geometry to prove source distance and relative pressure. Verify that center, quarter-angle, and side positions map continuously through the public Acoustic Propagation Interface, and that Distance Loss, Stereo Spread, and Distance Air Damping each reach an observable bypass state.

Acoustic-factor evidence

Through the Acoustic Factors Interface, require one complete default entry per displayed factor, bounded 0–100% amounts, copy-safe normalization, and an effective zero when any factor is switched off. In-browser inspection verifies all twenty-three rows contain both native controls, each surface and Wet Microtexture can be isolated, and Reset restores the current target preset.

Render-loop evidence

Prove through the Render Loop Interface that repeated wake requests schedule one frame, stopped state settles after one draw, and active state never draws above 30 frames per second. Browser profiling compares idle callbacks and canvas work before and after the change.

Analysis evidence

A known 1 kHz signal must report a centroid near 1 kHz with negligible high-band energy and low flatness. Known synthetic bursts must produce the expected prominent-onset count under the shared detector. The implemented pass also measures sample kurtosis and 5/20/100/500 ms envelope distributions. Single-drop verification additionally derives peak time and cumulative-energy timing directly from returned Rain Impact Waveforms. A later visualizer extension may expose modulation spectrum, stereo mid/side energy, and interchannel coherence.

Listening acceptance

Level-matched randomized A/B listening remains necessary because aggregate metrics can preserve a perceptually worse result. The implemented checkpoint—70% Distance Loss, 20% Mid Texture, 60% Distance Air Damping, Compression off—must retain its field depth, compact contacts, and Redwood tonal balance while Wet Microtexture moves the result toward the locally continuous spray and needle-like detail preferred in Farnell’s 13–25 s interval. The bounded comparison toggles only Wet Microtexture at identical Arrivals, marks, propagation, and output loudness.

Reference-file evidence

Verify the Amazon WAV against its exact byte count and MD5 and the Redwood preview against its SHA-256, then prove a caller can resolve and load either manifest entry through the Rain Reference Interface. In a real browser, verify selector-driven metrics and provenance, populated static canvases, explicit Start consent, Source Mix endpoints and midpoint, looping selected Reference Playback, clean Stop, and no console error. A supported local file becomes a silent analysis override without upload.

Source-mix evidence

Through the Source Mix Interface, prove exact generated-only and Reference-only endpoints, √½ gains at 50%, continuous bounded values, and clamping outside the slider range.

Time-stretch evidence

Through the Reference Playback Interface, prove that equal Speed and explicit comparison rate produce 1× playback, 0.75× and 2× requests remain exact, extremes are visibly limited to 0.75×–4×, and every supported media pitch-preservation flag is enabled.

06 · Scope

This is a perceptual simulator, not yet a luminaire model.

The browser prototype establishes the temporal and interaction language. It does not claim radiometric, colorimetric, electrical, thermal, or psychoacoustic fidelity.

07 · Further notes

Poisson is the clockwork underneath the organism.

The project’s deeper ambition remains a continuous stochastic light process whose statistics resemble naturally occurring physical phenomena. Every generated impact begins at a Steady Poisson Arrival, receives one coherent audio-only Rain Mark, and is exactly summed into the output. Drop Population enriches event marks without becoming another clock; its optional link to Speed is a control relationship, not weather modulation. Rain References remain visible evaluation and playback sources, but generated audio stays Pure Synthesis and never consumes their samples or derived waveforms. Research direction: physical real-time rain impact synthesis, stochastic sound-atom and block-synthesis architecture, compact parabolic and nonlinear Pure Data rain pulses, contact-time Gaussian-windowed generated-noise grains, measured airborne drop impacts on different surfaces, and intensity-dependent physical and statistical rain-sound synthesis. Conversation direction: Poisson Light Evolution and Spectrographs of Rain Sound.
