01 · Problem statement

Random light usually looks like software pretending to be alive.

Conventional flicker effects repeat, quantize, or call attention to their algorithm. Pwason needs a perceptually calm, nonrepeating process whose timing and physical envelopes feel closer to steady rain than to a lighting preset. The calibrated 1,000-Arrival field now matches Redwood’s foreground tempo, continuity, centroid, and high-band share closely enough that the remaining work is shape rather than density. Redwood is the sole enforceable acoustic target. Farnell remains useful as a deliberately brighter, more impulsive secondary procedural diagnostic, but it must never pull the default away from Redwood or be averaged into a fictitious target. Pwason does not need bubbles, more light Channels, a second clock, speculative secondary contacts, or a recording layer.

02 · Solution

Keep the clockwork simple. Let distance reveal the field.

The primary process remains fixed-rate homogeneous Poisson. Speed controls the total lamp Arrival rate, Drop Population independently controls the probability of fine, mixed, and large-drop-rich audio Rain Marks, and an optional Speed–Population Link moves the two controls together. A Reference Profile separately records its detected prominent-onset density and provisional equivalent total-Arrival rate; Redwood now uses 38.5 detected onsets/s and a field-continuity calibration of 1,000 total Arrivals/s. The Generated Rain Renderer keeps one causal response per Arrival. At the calibrated default, roughly eight thousand exact Arrivals occupy the eight-second analysis profile, most remote and quiet, while the small near subset retains foreground contrast. Exact Poisson overlap—not a recording, stationary bed, bubble model, sustained per-drop hiss, or second clock—produces the complete texture.

04 · Implementation decisions

Two deep stochastic Modules; thin sensory Adapters.

The Poisson Engine owns steady timing, Channel routing, and independent Impact Position behind one small Arrival Interface. The Generated Rain Renderer separately owns audio-only Rain Mark sampling, pure waveform generation, propagation, and exact accumulation. This preserves one high testing seam and prevents drop acoustics, LED wavelength, or block-rendering details from leaking into the primary timing process.

Temporal primitive

Steady mode is homogeneous Poisson: inter-arrival gaps are sampled from an exponential distribution at fixed λ.

Logarithmic speed

The Speed slider stores a continuous base-10 exponent from 0 through 3. Equal travel spans map to 1–10, 10–100, and 100–1000 events per second; the engine receives the resulting rate, not the exponent.

Drop Population

A separate continuous primary slider moves from fine-dominant through mixed to large-drop-rich rain. Its value selects a probability distribution over audio Rain Marks; it does not change Λ, Channel routing, or LED envelopes while unlinked. “Drop Population” is canonical because one scalar controls a distribution rather than one literal drop diameter.

Speed–Population Link

A primary switch labeled “Link Speed + Drop Population” optionally keeps both sliders at the same normalized position. Speed normalizes as u = log₁₀(Λ)/3; Drop Population spans the same u ∈ [0,1]. Linking is bidirectional: moving either control updates the other, enabling the link aligns them immediately, and disabling it preserves both current values. The default keeps it off so the calibrated 1,000/s field retains the independently selected 69% Drop Population. This relationship never changes Channel Coupling and never makes λ time-varying.

High-rate scheduling

A short-interval scheduler prepares Arrivals through a 50 ms Web Audio lookahead, avoiding browser minimum-timeout distortion while preserving each Poisson timestamp. Event counting and LEDs activate on wall time. Decorative trails are capped at 30 per second to bound DOM animation and GPU work.

Total rate

Speed is the total distinct physical Arrival rate Λ for the lamp. The number of hardware Channels never multiplies the underlying rain density, and an onset detector does not redefine Speed: it observes only the subset of Arrivals that remain prominent after Rain Mark level, surface, distance, damping, and overlap.

Event marks

Each Arrival owns amplitude, attack, decay, instantaneous rate, a routing kind, an energy-normalized eight-element Channel vector, and one continuous Impact Position. Position is independent of Channel routing.

Channel Coupling

Shared Arrivals occur at CΛ. Eight private streams each occur at (1−C)Λ/8. Their superposition remains Λ; a shared Arrival still counts once.

Listening Field

The prototype models a flat circular field around a listener h = 1.5 m above the impact plane. Field Depth continuously maps its enabled amount to a 1–100 m radius, defaults to 44% or 44.6 m, and collapses positions beneath the listener when disabled. Speed remains the total Arrival rate inside the selected field; widening it changes the distribution of audible distance, not Arrival density.

Distance field

Distance Loss δ maps each Arrival’s geometric h/d pressure through the exponent γ = δ/0.7. Bypass δ = 0 preserves unity pressure, the accepted 70% Redwood setting is the ordinary free-field h/d law, and larger amounts become progressively steeper. Remote events remain nonzero exact Arrivals rather than a free-running bed, statistical block approximation, or additional event clock.

Spatial distribution

Every Arrival samples r = R√U and an independent uniform azimuth. The square root distributes impacts uniformly by surface area, producing more distant than nearby events without discrete distance bands or additional Channels.

Acoustic Propagation

Source distance is d = √(r²+h²), and full Distance Loss is h/d, normalized to 1 directly beneath the listener. Azimuth is folded into pan = (2/π) asin(sin θ), then scaled by Stereo Spread so equal angular steps produce equal pan steps without binary left/right placement. Distance Air Damping applies a distance-dependent low-pass response. This pass still adds no propagation delay, reflections, occlusion, reverberation, or binaural head model.

Rain Impact Waveform

Each response is finite, deterministic for its seed, and behaviorally brief. A sub-millisecond signed Direct Contact begins near the Arrival and a compact rounded surface-contact window supplies the event-local excitation. Leaf contacts use smooth parabolic or quartic pressure-like shaping with restrained generated roughness; litter or soil contacts use a compact Gaussian-like window over analytically generated colored noise. Default variants peak inside the compact surface-contact window, with the 90th-percentile peak no later than 25 ms, have a median 90%-energy time no greater than 35 ms, and keep the 90th percentile no greater than 55 ms. Their energy envelopes must not retain the strongly right-skewed fast-attack/exponential-noise morphology being replaced. Exact zero-valued endpoints prevent buffer seams. The Generated Rain Renderer maintains 192 reusable seeded variants for bounded live cost.

Pure-synthesis Rain Mark model

The Generated Rain Renderer deterministically assigns each caller-owned Arrival an audio-only Rain Mark drawn from Drop Population. One mark coherently relates drop-size class, velocity proxy, impact level, contact duration, target surface, surface damping, broad spectral emphasis, bounded response-region center and bandwidth variation, and surface-envelope variation. Spectral emphasis is a correlated material property rather than eight unrelated random gains. Every default mark retains a low/mid Surface Body. Upper-frequency Surface Detail may occur on any mark and is not assigned to a diameter class. Its allowance tightens when a source response becomes foreground-prominent, which is a Redwood-calibrated perceptual coherence rule rather than a physical size-to-frequency law. The foreground allowance is material-specific: litter retains a broader noisy contact than leaf or wood, while all complete-profile Redwood constraints remain authoritative. The initial target contains leaves, leaf litter or soil, and a restrained wood contribution; liquid surfaces, entrained bubbles, and delayed secondary contacts are excluded.

Generated Surface Response

The audio implementation retains eight overlapping, nominal ERB-derived candidate regions centered at approximately 100, 477, 990, 1,877, 4,089, 6,051, 9,289, and 18,500 Hz. Each Rain Mark deterministically offsets every center by at most 0.28 nominal ERB steps and varies bandwidth by at most 18% under Event Variation; the upper nominal center remains capped at 18.5 kHz. This prevents the complete population from preserving eight fixed filter peaks without turning one impact broadband or adding another Acoustic Factor. A default Rain Mark selects two regions: at least one of the four body regions and one detail region when that group is enabled. Spectral Sparsity is an explicit switchable Acoustic Factor: zero leaves all candidate regions active and increasing it reduces per-mark occupancy. Selection probabilities preserve aggregate Redwood contours. Detail-to-body balance is calculated from expected stochastic output energy—coefficient gain multiplied by the square root of each varied filter’s effective noise bandwidth—so an extremely wide upper filter cannot masquerade as equal-energy merely because its coefficient matches a narrow body filter. Foreground-prominent marks retain nonzero detail under a tighter allowance; this is not another Acoustic Factor, event clock, propagation effect, or global low-pass. Each selected region receives generated excitation only inside the compact surface-contact window; afterward its filter evolves at zero input. Leaf responses remain broadly papery across a population; litter remains darker but retains upper detail; the restrained default wood contribution remains firmer but non-tonal. Low, Mid, and High Texture scale candidate groups. Band Independence varies generated noise plus finite within-contact envelope motion without mapping audio regions to LED Channels or introducing a second Arrival. A synthesized 20 kHz output bandwidth limit remains. No recording, recorded grain, impulse response, extracted texture waveform, stable note, stationary background bed, sustained per-drop noise feed, or hidden modal tone contributes to generated audio.

Wet Microtexture

Wet Microtexture is an explicit switchable Acoustic Factor inside the Rain Impact Waveform Implementation. While the finite surface-contact window is open, generated noise is smoothed into a correlated control signal, positive excursions above a moderate threshold are rectified and quadratically shaped, and a high-pass stage followed by a short seed-varied band-pass wavelet converts them into signed pressure-like microtransients. The default must keep the median Rain Impact crest below 1.35 times the bypass population so Wet Microtexture remains cushioned by the Surface Response; higher slider values may deliberately expose sharper cusps. Its timing is deterministic from the parent Rain Mark seed, ends with that response, and does not create secondary contacts, additional Arrivals, a hidden event scheduler, a stationary noise bed, or a liquid/bubble interpretation.

Poisson Shot Synthesis

Generated audio is x(t) = Σ h(t−tᵢ;Mᵢ): the exact sample-wise superposition of Rain Impact Waveforms at caller-owned Steady Poisson timestamps. Sparse rates expose individual responses; dense rates become noise-like through overlap. There is no second clock, generic ambient-noise layer, granular playback, or Cox modulation.

Generated Rain Renderer

One deep Module is the sole live/offline seam for generated rain. It maps caller-owned Arrivals and the selected Drop Population to Rain Marks, waveforms, gain, continuous spatial presentation, distance filtering, and output samples while never creating or moving a Poisson timestamp. The AudioWorklet is a thin accumulation Adapter over prepared renderer plans, not a competing synthesis path.

Exact block accumulation

Generated impacts accumulate in continuous Web Audio render blocks instead of constructing thousands of short-lived nodes. This is an exact computational partition of the same Poisson sum, not a distant-field approximation: response and filter state cross block boundaries, event IDs never reseed per block, no block is looped or normalized, and no Arrival is capped or dropped. Tests prove that changing block size produces bit-identical offline output.

Acoustic Factors Interface

Nineteen named factors are the complete adjustable generated-audio surface: Impact Body, Impact Softness, Event Variation, Low/Mid/High Texture, Leaf Surface, Litter / Soil, Wood Surface, Band Independence, Spectral Sparsity, Wet Microtexture, Surface Diversity, Distance Loss, Field Depth, Stereo Spread, Distance Air Damping, Density Compensation, and Compression. Each has one native enable switch and one continuous 0–100% amount slider. Tail Length, Surface Sustain, Micro-splashes, and Splash Delay are removed because endpoint sweeps produced negligible field change and added shallow Interface surface. Surface duration remains an internal Rain Mark and surface property. A debounced rebuild updates future generated waveform buffers without interrupting the Poisson engine.

Listening checkpoint

The Redwood-first default uses 8% Impact Body, 90% Impact Softness, 90% Low Texture, 20% Mid Texture, 32% High Texture, 98% Band Independence, 92% Spectral Sparsity, 45% Wet Microtexture, 70% Distance Loss, 44% Field Depth, 45% Distance Air Damping, Wood Surface on at 20%, and Compression off. Native-rate diagnostics report approximately 3.66 kHz centroid, 19.9% energy above 8 kHz, 15.5× crest, 49.2% envelope CV, 65.4% background floor, 0.56 band sync, 8.5 kurtosis, 0.27 100 ms CV, and 35.8 detected onsets/s. In a native 48 kHz browser comparison this is approximately 1.8 dB Perceptual Profile and 2.4 dB Spectral Distribution distance to Redwood inside its 80 Hz–18 kHz Reference Evaluation Passband, versus 5.8 dB and 8.7 dB to the secondary Farnell diagnostic over its full shared passband. The retained raw 80 Hz–20 kHz Redwood trace remains approximately 6.0 dB because its last two points expose the preview codec cutoff. Under the shared q90 Strong policy, the generated Strong microscope measures approximately 2.43 kHz centroid and 12.0% energy above 8 kHz; the browser-selected Redwood Strong at 7.680 s remains approximately 2.97 kHz and 15.3%. These values are regenerated from the complete preset and remain measured checkpoints rather than physical constants.

Compression policy

The next generated-audio baseline bypasses artistic dynamics compression so Rain Mark level and distance remain audible. Output protection may prevent clipping, but it must not pump, raise tails, or act as a hidden texture control. The current switchable Compression Acoustic Factor remains visible until implementation replaces that path.

Source Mix

A continuous 0–100% control defaults to generated-only and crossfades toward the selected Reference Playback. Gains follow cosine and sine of the half-quadrant: 0% is generated-only, 50% gives both sources √½ gain, and 100% is Reference-only. Short Web Audio gain ramps prevent slider zipper noise.

Reference Playback

The selected bundled Rain Reference loops only after the user presses Start, only while the process is running and audio is enabled, and only when Source Mix assigns it nonzero gain. A profile-declared interval also bounds playback, so Farnell loops only 14–24 seconds rather than the complete WAV. Stop or disabling audio pauses the loop. A user-selected local Rain Reference remains analysis-only and never replaces Reference Playback.

Pitch-preserving time stretch

Reference Playback rate is Speed divided by the selected profile’s equivalent total-Arrival rate when that independent calibration exists; it must never divide total Speed by detected foreground onsets while presenting the result as a physical-density match. The HTML media Adapter explicitly enables standard pitch preservation so transport changes do not transpose or spectrally shift the selected Rain Reference. Steady rain is statistically stationary, so transport changes may be hard to perceive and are not equivalent to event-separated density resynthesis. Operator listening found distortion below 0.75×; the clean comparison range remains 0.75×–4×, with visible limiting outside it.

Output level

A final gain control ranges from 25% to 250% and defaults to 160%. The live oscilloscope taps the signal after this stage.

Output oscilloscope

An AnalyserNode taps the complete post-output-stage Source Mix immediately before the audio destination. The scope reports the browser’s combined digital output, not acoustic room or eardrum pressure.

Bounded Render Loop

The visual Render Loop draws once while stopped, wakes on state changes, continues only while the process or visible tails are active, and caps drawing at 30 frames per second. The oscilloscope emits at most one plotted point per CSS pixel. Audio scheduling remains independent and preserves every Arrival timestamp.

Reference Library

The default profile is Andron827’s CC0 stereo field recording of steady rainfall on earthen ground, fallen logs, and large leaves in Redwood Shores, chosen because its source description contains no stream or flowing water. The bundled file is Freesound’s openly retrievable high-quality MP3 preview of the original 44.1 kHz, 16-bit WAV and is labeled as such. Its Reference Profile declares an 18 kHz evaluation ceiling because the preview encoder cuts the signal above that point; 18–20 kHz remains visible and explicitly unscored rather than becoming a synthetic low-pass target. The exact 60-second Amazon light-rain WAV from Xavier et al.’s study remains selectable with its DOI, CC BY 4.0 license, original filename, byte count, and MD5. Andy Farnell’s 47.22-second, stereo, 44.1 kHz, 16-bit procedural rain WAV is the third profile; provenance, checksum, selected interval, and the absence of standalone license terms are explicit.

Reference Profile calibration

Every profile stores detected prominent-onset density separately from equivalent total-Arrival rate. Redwood’s current ten-second browser analysis detects 38.5 onsets/s; Amazon retains its separately measured 15.8 onsets/s value; and Farnell’s declared 14–24 second interval measures 43.4 onsets/s. The shared detector uses 256-sample RMS windows, 128-sample hops at 48 kHz, positive log-energy flux above its 85th percentile, local maxima, and a 10 ms refractory interval. These are not claims about true physical-drop counts. Joint onset, background-floor, tonal, and listening comparison provisionally assigns Redwood 1,000 total Arrivals/s, giving a prominence fraction of about 3.85%; Amazon and Farnell remain explicitly uncalibrated rather than inheriting that multiplier.

Rain Diagnostics Module

One deep Rain Diagnostics Interface accepts a complete mono field plus sample rate and returns its complete Profile Analysis, one Representative Field Window, up to three Impact Microscope choices, one Onset Population, prominent onsets, and a time-order-independent spectral distribution. Generated, selected-reference, local-override, and Farnell callers all cross this same Seam. The Module owns selection, alignment, overlap suppression, and population-bounding policy so no caller can silently substitute a fixed favorite moment or largest absolute sample.

Onset Population

The Rain Diagnostics Module selects at most 96 evenly distributed prominent onsets from each complete profile, extracts 120 ms around each with 20 ms preroll, reduces every excerpt to 240 half-millisecond RMS points, subtracts its median pre-onset floor, and independently normalizes its post-onset peak. The visual reports q10–q90, q50, and the q50 peak and 90%-energy delays. Signed waveforms are intentionally not averaged because unrelated stochastic phase would cancel rather than reveal contact morphology.

Representative Field selection

Each source declares only its complete analysis interval: eight seconds for generated rain, at most ten seconds for ordinary references, and exactly 14–24 seconds for Farnell. The Rain Diagnostics Module scans one-second candidates every 10 ms, independently level-normalizes each candidate spectrum, and chooses the candidate with minimum RMS dB distance from that source’s complete normalized profile. Deterministic earliest-candidate tie breaking keeps selection reproducible. Window Δ exposes the winning distance; the complete profile remains authoritative.

Impact Microscope selection and alignment

The shared prominent-onset detector identifies observable acoustic onsets in every complete profile and scores each by its first 40 ms of post-onset energy. Descending-energy nonmaximum suppression removes every candidate less than one 120 ms microscope window from a stronger candidate. The remaining distinct contacts are ranked by energy: Strong is nearest the 90th percentile, Typical is nearest the median, and Soft is nearest the 25th percentile. The absolute maximum is deliberately excluded from Strong when enough contacts exist, preventing a rare wood-like thump or other outlier from defining normal rain morphology. Every choice places its detected onset 20 ms into the excerpt, leaving pre-onset context and 100 ms of response; sources with fewer distinct contacts expose only the available choices. The waveform and spectrogram draw the same dashed marker and report choice, onset time, and peak delay. Alignment accuracy is bounded by the detector’s approximately 2.7 ms hop; it aligns observable acoustic evidence and does not claim the physical collision time is known inside a field recording. If no onset is detectable, the microscope visibly reports one peak fallback.

Microscope Scaling Module

One display-only Microscope Scaling Interface accepts every available source’s complete-profile RMS and all of its Impact Microscopes, then returns per-source waveform and spectrogram gains plus an optional shared STFT power reference. Profile-matched is the default: each source receives one gain that brings its complete profile to a common RMS baseline; that unchanged gain applies to Strong, Typical, and Soft. The maximum 99.5th-percentile amplitude across all profile-scaled microscopes maps to 84% waveform height, while the maximum profile-scaled STFT power across the same population defines one shared 70 dB color reference. Because both references use all available choices, changing Strong, Typical, or Soft cannot silently rescale the display. Shape instead leaves every waveform and spectrogram independently normalized. Raw recording amplitude is not exposed as an apples-to-apples mode because microphone gain is uncalibrated. Microscope Scaling never changes stored samples, metrics, synthesis, Reference Playback, or Impact Audition.

Impact Audition

Each populated Impact Microscope exposes one native play button. A single diagnostic playback path independently peak-matches the selected mono excerpt to 32% full scale, adds four-millisecond edge fades to prevent excerpt-boundary clicks, and plays exactly the visible 120 ms through the current Output Level. Starting an audition is an explicit user audio gesture; it does not start the Poisson process, enter Source Mix, alter the stored diagnostic samples, or feed any Rain Reference into generated synthesis. Starting another Impact Audition replaces the previous one so diagnostic excerpts cannot stack.

Signal analysis

A dependency-free 512-point Hann-windowed FFT retains the established averaged power-spectrum metrics. A separate 1,024-point Hann-windowed STFT uses 4 ms hops for one-second Representative Field Windows and 1 ms hops for 120 ms Impact Microscopes. The canvas interpolates time and frequency samples onto a log-frequency, 70 dB, perceptually uniform color image; it does not enlarge discrete FFT cells into a block heatmap. Five-millisecond overlapping RMS frames also measure crest factor, envelope coefficient of variation, the 10th-percentile background floor relative to median, and mean envelope correlation across low, mid, and high regions. The long steady-profile pass omits display spectrogram frames to avoid needless memory retention.

Residual diagnostics

Generated and reference profile spectra are independently peak-normalized on one shared 80 Hz–20 kHz logarithmic grid. The raw 96-point Spectral Profile Residual remains visible as a faint hairline. The authoritative solid trace converts normalized dB back to relative power, applies a one-third-octave-FWHM Gaussian on the log-frequency grid, independently renormalizes both smoothed profiles, and subtracts reference dB from generated dB. Positive is generated excess and negative is generated deficit. Perceptual Profile Distance is the RMS of that solid trace only inside the selected Reference Evaluation Passband. The Spectral Distribution Residual computes the raw signed difference independently at q10, q25, q50, q75, and q90 of short-time spectral power after normalizing each complete profile, and its RMS uses the same passband. Discarding frame order makes the comparison valid for independent stochastic realizations while preserving quiet-floor, median-texture, and foreground-impact mismatch. Frequencies outside the selected passband remain visible, shaded, and unscored.

Redwood Acoustic Target Profile

The generated comparison uses Redwood’s provisional 1,000 total Arrivals/s rather than its 38.5 detected-onset baseline. The first ten seconds define three independently peak-normalized constraints. The broad target uses nine bands over 80–20,000 Hz with edges at 80, 160, 315, 630, 1,250, 2,500, 5,000, 10,000, 16,000, and 20,000 Hz and levels −4.8, −2.8, 0, −1, −5.7, −8.2, −4.2, −4.8, and −2.6 dB; generated distance must remain below 3 dB RMS. The fine target uses thirteen bands with edges at 80, 375, 750, 1,125, 1,688, 2,438, 3,375, 4,500, 6,000, 7,875, 10,125, 12,750, 15,750, and 19,500 Hz and levels −1.6, 0, −4.1, −7.4, −10.3, −12, −13.9, −12.1, −11.1, −7.3, −10.8, −8.1, and −3.3 dB; generated distance must remain below 2 dB RMS. A third 23-center one-third-octave perceptual contour spans 100 Hz–16 kHz inside Redwood’s reliable preview passband and must remain below 1.8 dB RMS. Diagnostic ranges are 3–6.5 kHz centroid, 12–30% energy above 8 kHz, 0.005–0.16 spectral flatness, 8–18× crest, 0.3–0.7 envelope CV, at least 64% background floor, less than 0.8 band sync, 5.5–16 kurtosis, and 0.18–0.45 100 ms CV. With Wood Surface enabled at 20%, the seeded calibrated field currently measures approximately 3.66 kHz centroid, 19.9% above 8 kHz, 65.4% background floor, 15.5× crest, 8.5 kurtosis, and 35.8 detected onsets/s against Redwood’s approximately 4.22 kHz, 23.9%, 72%, 14.4×, 9.5, and 38.5/s. These values establish statistical convergence; exact time-aligned spectrogram pixels are neither expected nor desirable from independent stochastic realizations.

Farnell diagnostic profile

Farnell’s 14–24 second interval is a secondary Procedural Rain Reference, not measured rainfall and never an automatically enforced target. Native-rate analysis measures approximately 9.10 kHz centroid, 55.5% energy above 8 kHz, 0.813 spectral flatness, 86.3× crest, 1.11 envelope CV, 71% background floor, 0.46 band sync, 683 kurtosis, and 43 detected onsets/s. Those values conflict materially with Redwood’s darker, calmer field. Farnell remains useful for revealing how reduced dry contact and generated noise-band excitation affect perceived wetness, but it cannot override Redwood tuning or be averaged into the primary target.

Static comparison

The visual lab renders in one vertical reading order: aggregate normalized spectra; signed complete-profile and distributional residuals; Generated, selected-reference, and Farnell Representative Field Windows; one global Microscope Scaling control; three source Impact Microscope cards with independent Strong, Typical, Soft, and Impact Audition controls; then three aligned Onset Populations. Field cards retain complete-profile metrics alongside their local waveforms and spectrograms. Static analysis renders only on initial load, Speed, Rain Reference, Drop Population or Acoustic Factor changes, a microscope-choice or scaling-mode change, or a debounced resize; it never joins the live animation loop. The displayed generated total rate follows Speed from 1–1,000 Arrivals/s, while each Reference Profile separately retains its interval, detected-onset rate, equivalent-total calibration, and residual diagnostics.

05 · Testing decisions

Verify what a caller can observe—not how randomness is assembled.

The Poisson Engine Interface continues to prove the unchanged steady clock and light routing. The Generated Rain Renderer remains the highest synthesis seam: its seeded outputs must prove Drop Population behavior, pure synthesis, physically coherent response populations, live/offline agreement, and block-partition invariance without exposing internal random draws or filter topology.

Good test

A known seed and settings produce the same observable sequence after any internal refactor.

Statistical sanity

A long fixed-rate run has positive gaps and a mean inter-arrival time close to 1/λ.

Browser evidence

Inspect start/stop, sound consent, live meter updates, all eight LEDs, logarithmic decade markers and intermediate Speed values, reduced motion, and narrow layouts.

High-rate evidence

At 1000 events per second, compare the event count against elapsed wall time to prove the scheduler does not collapse to the browser’s minimum timer frequency.

Signal-path evidence

Verify that the analyser receives the complete post-output-stage Source Mix, displays silence before Start, shows both sources at the midpoint, and returns toward silence after Stop.

Rain-impact evidence

Verify through the Rain Impact Waveform Interface that complete generated responses contain positive and negative pressure-like samples, remain bounded and deterministic, and begin and end at zero. Across the complete default response bank, require the 90th-percentile peak no later than 25 ms, a median 90%-energy time no greater than 35 ms, and a 90th-percentile 90%-energy time no greater than 55 ms. The population’s median temporal-energy skew must remain below 1.5 so a fast attack cannot hide a long exponential stochastic tail. The known high-energy large Rain Marks exposed by the earlier maximum-energy microscope audit must each remain below 10 kHz centroid, 55% energy above 8 kHz, and 0.45 first-difference roughness over their first 40 ms when rendered alone, proving the originating response—not overlap or display normalization—has been corrected. Across all 192 default variants, the 95th percentile of energy above 8 kHz must remain below 90% and every variant below 95%, proving Surface Detail remains finite without erasing papery high-frequency marks. In the seeded 1,000-Arrival/s Redwood field, require q90 Strong to remain within 2.0–2.6 kHz centroid and 10–16% energy above 8 kHz while the complete profile still satisfies the broad, fine-band, and perceptual Redwood constraints. With Direct Contact bypassed, enabling Wet Microtexture must measurably increase high-passed microtransient contrast over its bypass; at the default amount its median full-response crest must remain below 1.35 times bypass so it does not replace the audible Surface Response. Prove disabling Impact Body, every texture region, and Wet Microtexture yields literal silence rather than hidden ambience, and prove leaf and litter profiles remain observably distinct without periodic resonators.

Generated-renderer evidence

Through the Generated Rain Renderer Interface, prove one Arrival produces a deterministic Rain Mark and render plan with reusable response-bank identity, gain, pan, and distance-filter values; the renderer snapshots caller settings; and offline profiles require caller-owned Arrivals. This keeps Poisson timing outside the renderer and makes live, AudioWorklet, and analytical composition share one tested seam.

Rate-calibration evidence

Prove that a Reference Profile keeps detected foreground-onset density distinct from equivalent total-Arrival rate, that Redwood compares at 1,000 total Arrivals/s while retaining 38.5 detected onsets/s, and that uncalibrated Amazon and Farnell profiles never silently inherit Redwood’s multiplier. Apply the same prominent-onset detector to synthetic and decoded profile samples, and display which rate drives generation and pitch-preserving playback.

Drop-Population evidence

At a fixed Speed and seed, moving Drop Population must preserve every Arrival timestamp, Channel route, and Impact Position while observably shifting the generated Rain Mark distribution from fine-dominant toward large-drop-rich responses. At a fixed unlinked Drop Population, moving Speed changes event density without silently changing the selected mark distribution.

Link evidence

Prove the Speed–Population Link at both endpoints and intermediate logarithmic Speed values: enabling aligns normalized positions immediately, moving either slider updates the other bidirectionally, disabling preserves both values, and no link operation changes Channel Coupling. Browser evidence verifies accessible names and visible linked state.

Rain-Mark coherence

Through renderer output populations rather than internal draws, prove that fixed seeded Rain Marks reproduce exactly and that increasingly large-drop-rich populations shift the distributions of impact energy, contact duration, and surface response together. Across a seeded default population, require broad non-monotonic spectral diversity, meaningful upper-band energy, and event spectra that vary while their aggregate remains complete. Switching Spectral Sparsity off must audibly and measurably broaden individual responses without changing Poisson timing. At the complete renderer seam, isolated Leaf Surface profiles must remain within 4–9 kHz centroid and 20–60% energy above 8 kHz; isolated Litter / Soil profiles must remain within 1–5 kHz and 3–30%, with leaf remaining at least 1 kHz and five percentage points brighter. These are anti-extreme guardrails, not claims that one field recording isolates either surface. No test exposes private band coefficients.

Tonal-target evidence

Render the seeded default 1,000 Arrivals/s field through the same Generated Rain Renderer used by live playback. Independently peak-normalize the documented nine-band broad, thirteen-band fine, and 23-center one-third-octave perceptual profiles, then require less than 3 dB, 2 dB, and 1.8 dB RMS distance respectively from the measured Redwood contours. Require at least 65% background floor while keeping envelope CV, crest, kurtosis, 100 ms CV, and detected-onset density inside their documented ranges. This constrains tone and field continuity through one public Interface while keeping the recording outside synthesis.

Pure-synthesis evidence

For identical Arrivals, Drop Population, Acoustic Factors, and seed, changing or removing every Rain Reference must leave generated samples bit-identical. Generated playback must make no request for a recording asset. Static dependency inspection and the public Renderer Interface together prove that recordings, grains, impulse responses, and extracted texture waveforms cannot enter synthesis.

Block-partition null test

Render one seeded Arrival sequence through the exact reference summation and through continuous blocks of several sizes, including contacts and tails crossing every boundary. Subtract the outputs and require only floating-point accumulation noise. The test also proves persistent response and filter state, stable event seeding, no per-block normalization, no repeated blocks, and no dropped Arrivals.

Spatial evidence

Across a long seeded run, all Impact Positions remain inside the configured Listening Field and the mean squared radius approaches half the field’s squared radius, as required by uniform area sampling.

Propagation evidence

Use a known 3–4–5 geometry at 70% Distance Loss to prove source distance and h/d relative pressure. At that same setting, prove both a 1.5 m and a 66 m Arrival exactly follow h/d and the near Arrival is more than thirty times stronger. Verify that center, quarter-angle, and side positions map continuously and that Distance Loss, Stereo Spread, and Distance Air Damping each reach an observable bypass state.

Acoustic-factor evidence

Through the Acoustic Factors Interface, require exactly nineteen complete default entries, bounded 0–100% amounts, copy-safe normalization, an effective zero when any factor is switched off, and the absence of the four removed factor ids. In-browser inspection verifies all nineteen rows contain both native controls, each surface and Wet Microtexture can be isolated, and Reset restores the Redwood-first target preset.

Render-loop evidence

Prove through the Render Loop Interface that repeated wake requests schedule one frame, stopped state settles after one draw, and active state never draws above 30 frames per second. Browser profiling compares idle callbacks and canvas work before and after the change.

Analysis evidence

A known 1 kHz signal must report a centroid near 1 kHz with negligible high-band energy and low flatness. Known synthetic bursts must produce the expected prominent-onset count under the shared detector. A constructed three-part field with low-only, mixed, and high-only seconds must select the mixed second as its Representative Field Window. A ten-contact field with known increasing energies must return Strong, Typical, and Soft microscopes at the 90th-percentile, median, and 25th-percentile energies, exclude the absolute maximum from Strong, keep selected onsets at least 120 ms apart, and place every detected onset 20 ms into its excerpt. A multi-contact field must return an Onset Population with q10/q50/q90 envelopes, a shared 20 ms marker, and positive peak and 90%-energy delays. A steady tone and a level-varying copy must retain a near-zero profile residual at the tone while producing negative quiet-percentile and positive loud-percentile distribution residuals. A two-tone fixture proves the raw full-grid mismatch remains visible while Perceptual Profile and Spectral Distribution distances exclude evidence above a declared Reference Evaluation Passband; null or absent ceilings retain the complete shared grid. Reference preparation must downmix only the declared interval and return all diagnostic views. Signal Visualization must robustly normalize quiet signals, accept a declared shared waveform gain, preserve per-pixel extrema, draw shared onset markers, and keep silence finite.

Microscope-scaling evidence

Through the Microscope Scaling Interface, a worked two-source fixture proves Shape requests independent panel normalization and Profile-matched derives exactly one waveform and spectrogram gain from each complete-profile RMS. The same fixture proves both impacts from one source inherit the same gain, the shared waveform reference comes from the largest profile-scaled robust peak, the shared spectrogram reference comes from the largest profile-scaled STFT power, and an unknown mode fails visibly. Browser evidence verifies Profile-matched is the initial global state, changing Shape and back updates the explanatory status and materially changes canvas pixels without changing the selected impact or audio state.

Impact-audition evidence

Through the Impact Audition Interface, prove that a nonzero excerpt is copied rather than mutated, level-matched to its declared target, tapered to literal zero at both excerpt boundaries, and returned at its declared sample rate. Silence must remain finite silence. In-browser evidence verifies all three buttons remain disabled until their matching microscope is populated, each click plays only that excerpt after an explicit audio gesture, the current Output Level applies, and a new audition replaces any one still active.

Listening acceptance

Level-matched randomized A/B listening remains necessary because aggregate metrics can preserve a perceptually worse result. The implemented checkpoint—8% Impact Body, 90% Low Texture, 20% Wood Surface enabled, 98% Band Independence, 92% Spectral Sparsity, 70% Distance Loss, 45% Distance Air Damping, and Compression off—must retain compact contacts and the sole enforceable Redwood tonal and temporal target. Farnell may expose useful wet-texture contrasts, but it is a secondary no-regression diagnostic rather than an optimization objective.

Reference-file evidence

Verify the Amazon WAV against its exact byte count and MD5 and both Redwood and Farnell assets against their SHA-256 values, then prove a caller can resolve every manifest entry and Redwood's 18 kHz Reference Evaluation Passband through the Rain Reference Interface. In a real browser, verify the aggregate spectrum precedes residual diagnostics, Redwood's raw residual tail remains visibly shaded and unscored, three one-second Representative Field cards, one global Shape / Profile-matched control, three playable 120 ms Impact Microscope cards with Strong/Typical/Soft selectors, and three Onset Population panels; every canvas and metric populates; each field reports a selected center and Window Δ; every onset diagnostic shows the same 20 ms marker; selector-driven provenance remains visible; and Source Mix still honors explicit Start and Stop. A supported local file becomes a silent selected-analysis override without upload.

Source-mix evidence

Through the Source Mix Interface, prove exact generated-only and Reference-only endpoints, √½ gains at 50%, continuous bounded values, and clamping outside the slider range.

Time-stretch evidence

Through the Reference Playback Interface, prove that equal Speed and explicit comparison rate produce 1× playback, 0.75× and 2× requests remain exact, extremes are visibly limited to 0.75×–4×, and every supported media pitch-preservation flag is enabled.

06 · Scope

This is a perceptual simulator, not yet a luminaire model.

The browser prototype establishes the temporal and interaction language. It does not claim radiometric, colorimetric, electrical, thermal, or psychoacoustic fidelity.

07 · Further notes

Poisson is the clockwork underneath the organism.

The project’s deeper ambition remains a continuous stochastic light process whose statistics resemble naturally occurring physical phenomena. Every generated impact begins at a Steady Poisson Arrival, receives one coherent audio-only Rain Mark, and is exactly summed into the output. Drop Population enriches event marks without becoming another clock; its optional link to Speed is a control relationship, not weather modulation. Rain References remain visible evaluation and playback sources, but generated audio stays Pure Synthesis and never consumes their samples or derived waveforms. Research direction: physical real-time rain impact synthesis, stochastic sound-atom and block-synthesis architecture, compact parabolic and nonlinear Pure Data rain pulses, contact-time Gaussian-windowed generated-noise grains, measured airborne drop impacts on different surfaces, and intensity-dependent physical and statistical rain-sound synthesis. Conversation direction: Poisson Light Evolution and Spectrographs of Rain Sound.
