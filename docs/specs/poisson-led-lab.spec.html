01 · Problem statement

The optical hypothesis needs its own clean bench and control.

The existing prototype begins with an acoustic waveform and derives light from it. That makes the LED result inseparable from decisions made for sound. This experiment instead reveals what a bounded, positive, steady Poisson current field does before acoustic interpretation and compares it with a conventional deterministic PWM condition.

Current lacks authority

An audio-first path allows an acoustic decision to change the LED waveform. The optical experiment cannot then distinguish Poisson behavior from source-model behavior.

Rate is easily misread

Speed must continue to mean one total lamp rate. At the maximum, 48,000 Arrivals/s total becomes an expected 6,000 Arrivals/s per Channel—not 48 kHz on every Channel.

A monitor can hide convergence

Automatic level matching would keep high-density modulation loud even as the current becomes steadier. The monitor must preserve the current’s changing AC magnitude.

No control condition

Without a rate- and mean-matched PWM baseline, the experiment cannot distinguish a Poisson-specific result from ordinary high-frequency visual integration.

Report windows create false beats

A fixed 768-sample display average updates at 62.5 Hz. Unless Λ is a multiple of 500, that window contains a fractional number of PWM cycles, so its average oscillates before the browser resamples it again at the display refresh. Variable-refresh browsers can also alternate full and dropped animation frames, making a naïve refresh estimate jump between 60 and 120 Hz. The resulting meta-flicker is a presentation artefact, not PWM current.

02 · Solution

Run stochastic current beside a periodic control, then choose what to monitor.

Poisson emits exact Arrivals and uniformly marks one of eight Channels. PWM drives all eight Channels in phase at Λ/8 cycles/s. Both conditions advance continuously from the same Total Event Budget and Target Mean Current; PWM On Current trades on-state height against automatically derived duty without changing that mean. A refresh-aware PWM LED Presentation requires at least four browser frames per resolved cycle, progressively reduces illustrated modulation as that quality limit approaches, and uses integrated current at and above 15 Hz per Channel. A shared inverse display-transfer Adapter makes equal linear current target comparable screen luminance in both LED banks. Separate fixed-scale scopes always retain the authoritative 48 kHz signals.

04 · Implementation decisions

One comparison Module owns the complete signal contract.

The Poisson LED Lab Engine Interface advances two fixed-source condition engines from shared settings and exposes one monitor choice. The worklet adapts both rendered blocks to audio and diagnostic messages; the page adapts those messages to two LED banks, pixels, and controls.

Exact timing

Inter-arrival gaps are exponentially distributed. Multiple Arrivals may occur inside one 48 kHz sample interval and each contributes independently; sample coincidence never merges event identity or energy.

Matched PWM control

Every PWM Channel uses one shared phase and frequency Λ/8. PWM On Current selects the positive on-state current from the commanded Target Mean Current through 100%; duty is derived as Target Mean Current ÷ PWM On Current, so their product always preserves the shared mean. On-time is duty ÷ frequency and silence is (1 − duty) ÷ frequency; both remain read-only evidence because independently controlling them would redefine frequency and duty. At the lower On Current endpoint duty reaches 100% and the output becomes continuous reduced current. The eight cycle counts still sum to Λ. The bank header displays commanded mean, On Current, duty, measured display refresh, and PWM LED Presentation separately. PWM advances beside Poisson and ignores Current Fall Time because that control belongs only to Poisson Current Responses.

Monitor, not switch

Monitor Source selects which completed condition block feeds the metrics, audio scope, and audio output. Both current scopes continue independently and retain their histories. The selector does not start, stop, reset, or reconfigure either condition.

Uniform routing

Every Arrival receives one uniform Channel mark. Poisson splitting gives eight independent streams with expected rate Λ/8 and preserves Λ as the total lamp rate.

Positive response

An Arrival injects a positive exponential current response with selectable fall time τ. There are no spectral, spatial, acoustic, or event-morphology marks.

Mean-held charge

Event charge varies inversely with expected Channel rate and response duration. At high overlap, the smooth limiter’s expected output approaches Target Mean Current. Sparse bounded pulses cannot always reach that target and must report their actual mean honestly.

Full-DC limit

Below 100%, Poisson uses finite mean-held event charge and PWM uses the selected duty cycle. Exactly 100% is the explicit limit of both models: every Channel outputs continuous unit current while Poisson Arrivals and PWM phase continue advancing. Direct subtraction of the 100% target makes the Current Monitor exactly and immediately silent.

Smooth limit

Raw positive shot current maps through u/(1+u). The current remains continuous in value, nonnegative, and below 100%, without a hard clipping plateau.

White fusion

Aggregate White equals the arithmetic mean of the eight instantaneous Channel currents. It has no Arrivals, response state, or independent gain of its own.

Direct sound monitor

Every fused-current sample is translated vertically by subtracting the commanded Target Mean Current. This preserves the waveform point for point without a DC filter, history, decay, or frequency shaping. A fixed source factor then leaves Poisson at unity and attenuates PWM to one quarter, followed by explicit logarithmic manual base Monitor Gain. The base defaults to the 2× safe maximum at a 50% target, making effective gain 2× for Poisson and 0.5× for PWM. Its target-dependent bound is Gsafe = 1 ÷ max(Ī, 1 − Ī), so no possible bounded current sample can exceed browser full scale. Changing Target Mean Current updates the slider maximum and clamps its value immediately. The signal is copied equally to both speakers with no automatic normalization, limiting, or compression. Because sparse bounded Poisson current may not attain its target exactly, it may retain a small inaudible residual offset rather than altering the waveform to force a measured zero mean.

Three oscilloscopes

Dedicated Poisson and PWM Current Oscilloscopes simultaneously plot their Aggregate White currents on the same fixed 0–100% scale. The selected-source Audio Oscilloscope plots exact target-centered, pre-gain samples on a fixed −1 to +1 current-unit scale with a zero line. One timebase selects 1 s, 100 ms, 10 ms, or 1 ms for all three. Every scope draws connected per-pixel minimum and maximum envelopes, retains unresolved excursions, preserves same-sign plateaus, and performs no visual normalization.

Refresh-aware PWM presentation

The PWM LED Presentation Module measures requestAnimationFrame cadence and normalizes ordinary fractional timing such as 59.94 Hz to its nearby nominal display rate. A higher refresh capability is accepted only after four observations and then latched for the run; slower dropped frames cannot demote it. The resolved-quality ceiling is min(15 Hz, display refresh ÷ 4), requiring at least four browser frames per PWM cycle and capping eight-Channel resolved presentation at 120 total events/s. Below 70% of that ceiling, a presentation-only phase emits On Current or zero. Across the remaining 30%, smoothstep interpolation progressively mixes that resolved current toward PWM On Current × duty; at and above the ceiling it emits the exact complete-cycle current mean. This declared display antialiasing never enters the engine, scopes, metrics, audio, or hardware hypothesis.

Shared display transfer

The LED Display Transfer Adapter maps every bounded linear current presentation value through the approximate inverse sRGB relation D(I) = I^(1/2.2) before assigning the CSS intensity. Thus a 50% integrated current uses about 73% display code, whose emitted screen luminance is approximately the temporal average of alternating full-on and off frames. Both Poisson and PWM banks use the same Adapter. Accessible current readouts, engine samples, scopes, audio, and metrics remain linear and uncompensated; this is a display calibration, not optical signal processing.

Poisson frame integration

Every worklet sample contributes to the report-interval arithmetic mean displayed by each Poisson DOM LED. The PWM bank does not reuse those arbitrary report-window means. Neither bank applies CSS brightness interpolation. Measurements and oscilloscopes remain authoritative diagnostic views rather than inputs to either current engine.

Isolation

This standalone file and its Modules do not call, configure, or revise the existing generated-audio path. The two experiments share only durable domain definitions for Arrival, Channel, Speed, and Aggregate White.

05 · Research interpretation

Matched mean current is a control condition, not physical equivalence.

PWM On Current is a waveform-morphology control, not a brightness control. It deliberately removes commanded mean current as a confound while exposing how on-state height and duty change electrical stress and temporal modulation. Equal mean current does not guarantee equal optical emission, efficacy, temperature, lifetime, visibility, or camera behavior.

Firmware, brightness, color temperature, RGB selection, power supply, and product generation may change the optical waveform. Macroscopic solar control

At the time resolution relevant to human vision, cameras, and a practical photodiode measurement, unobscured sunlight is effectively continuous optical power. The closest electrical control in the planned hardware is therefore regulated low-ripple constant current. PWM and Poisson are comparison waveforms, not more literal models of the Sun merely because their rates are high.

Microscopic statistics are separate

Thermal sunlight is super-Poissonian at sufficiently narrow spatial, spectral, and temporal resolution: photons bunch within its coherence scale. Broadband detectors collect enormous numbers of independent modes and photons, so that structure is averaged almost completely away. A physical LED under steady current already emits individual photons stochastically; intentionally modulating its drive at kilohertz rates adds a macroscopic fluctuation that sunlight does not require.

Why sunlight looks steady

At roughly terrestrial daylight irradiance, even one square millimetre receives on the order of 10¹⁵ photons each second. Relative counting fluctuations shrink approximately as 1/√N, making them negligible over visual frames or millisecond measurements. Solar steadiness comes from enormous flux and detector integration, not from a hidden PWM-like carrier.

Eight Channels are an approximation

The eight selected LED spectra may approximate a desired daylight chromaticity and coarse band balance, but they cannot reproduce a continuous AM1.5 spectrum, Fraunhofer lines, atmospheric absorption bands, or the angular split between direct sun and diffuse sky. Aggregate White is a temporal current monitor, not proof of a solar spectral match.

Two independent experiments

Spectral fitting chooses Channel currents against a declared target spectrum and geometry. Temporal fitting minimizes optical modulation against a declared bandwidth and exposure. Changing spectral ratios must not be used to hide temporal ripple, and changing Arrival statistics must not be described as improving solar spectral fidelity.

Poisson interpretation boundary

The lab's Λ describes engineered electrical Arrivals whose positive current responses can overlap. It does not describe the Sun's photon flux, thermal coherence, or elementary photon arrivals. A 48 kHz total field is only 6 kHz expected per Channel and remains a measurable stochastic current process until physical optical evidence demonstrates otherwise.

What the control buys us

At one Target Mean Current, moving On Current from the target to 100% moves PWM from continuous reduced current toward taller, narrower full-off pulses. This is scientifically useful because it isolates morphology. In a production lamp it would normally be an engineering setpoint, not an end-user brightness control.

Electrical burden changes

At fixed mean, higher On Current raises RMS current and therefore resistive conduction loss. A Texas Instruments analysis gives the concrete same-average-load example of 400 mA at 50% duty producing twice the inductor conduction loss of continuous 200 mA. High peaks may also increase switching loss and instantaneous thermal stress. TI analog/PWM analysis

Current is not photons

LED flux and efficacy are nonlinear functions of instantaneous current. GaN devices commonly exhibit efficiency droop at high injection current, so D × Φ(Ion) need not equal Φ(Imean). A peer-reviewed driver study likewise warns that low duty with high on-current can cause temporal overheating and reduced luminous efficacy. GaN efficiency-droop study · IEEE power-electronics study

Reliability is not monotonic

Same-average-current ageing tests found that moderate pulsed bias could reduce degradation relative to DC, while duty below 20% and the resulting high peaks could cause rapid degradation or abrupt failure. The correct region is part-, package-, temperature-, pulse-width-, and driver-specific. DC versus pulsed HBLED study

Why true PWM exists

Conventional true PWM usually fixes On Current at a well-regulated operating point and changes duty to dim. It offers wide, accurate low-level dimming and avoids low-current regulation errors; keeping the on-state current fixed can also improve spectral consistency. Its cost is deep temporal modulation, switching loss, possible camera banding, and temporal-light artefacts.

Why analog and hybrid exist

Analog dimming changes continuous current amplitude. It avoids full-off modulation, acoustic switching artefacts, and the highest PWM conduction losses, but may have poorer deep-dimming accuracy and current-dependent color. Hybrid drivers use high-duty, reduced-amplitude operation over much of the range and reserve PWM for the deepest levels. TI driver comparison · Analog Devices PWM guidance

Timing has no hidden freedom

With Target Mean Current, On Current, and frequency selected, duty, On Time, and Off Time are fully determined. Making On Time and Off Time independent would change frequency or mean and invalidate the matched experiment. In an ordinary dimmer, duty controls brightness when On Current is fixed; in this lab it compensates On Current to hold mean constant.

No universal cutoff

There is no universal imperceptibility frequency. Visibility varies with modulation depth, waveform spectrum, duty, luminance, retinal position, eye or object motion, exposure, viewer sensitivity, and cameras. A flat constant-current waveform is the clearest no-modulation case; low-ripple analog current or hybrid current with a raised floor is preferable to full-off PWM when temporal artefacts must be minimized.

What “other waveforms” means

DOE’s non-PWM recommendation means current waveforms without deep periodic on/off oscillation: steady DC, analog constant-current dimming with low ripple, or hybrid/shaped drive that keeps a nonzero floor and low modulation depth. A smooth waveform may reduce high harmonics relative to a square wave, but shape alone is not a guarantee. Aperiodic Poisson current also needs time-domain evaluation and cannot inherit periodic-PWM thresholds. DOE Flicker Research · CIE TN 006:2016

Historical frequency guidance

IEEE 1789-2015 historically placed unrestricted 100% modulation above 1.25 kHz for its low-risk recommendation and above 3 kHz for its no-observable-effect recommendation; IEEE now lists that document as Inactive-Reserved. Those boundaries concern stated risk models, not guaranteed invisibility. DOE later recommended greater than 12 kHz—and possibly higher—or avoiding deep PWM. IEEE 1789 status · DOE summary of IEEE guidance

Modern visibility evidence

CIE’s 2016 note summarized then-known stroboscopic and phantom-array visibility up to 2.5 kHz. More recent PNNL-backed work evaluates phantom-array visibility from 80 Hz through 20 kHz, and sensitive observers or rapid eye movement can extend visibility well beyond ordinary direct-flicker thresholds. Thus 20 kHz is an evidence-informed engineering starting region, not an imperceptibility guarantee. 2024 PNNL phantom-array research

This lab’s 6 kHz control

The 48 kHz total event budget is not a 48 kHz PWM carrier. Because the matched control uses fPWM = Λ/8, its maximum is 6 kHz per Channel. That is useful for the matched stochastic comparison but cannot be claimed universally imperceptible. A production PWM carrier above the studied range would need to be separated from Λ and validated independently.

Poisson rate is not a carrier

Six thousand expected Arrivals/s on one Channel does not equal a periodic 6 kHz waveform. Poisson clustering and gaps distribute modulation energy across frequencies, including below the mean Arrival rate. Convergence must therefore be judged from emitted-light modulation and its spectrum rather than by comparing Λ/8 numerically with a PWM carrier.

Cinema-class reference

Professional fixtures commonly expose frequency as an operating mode rather than a brand promise. ETC provides 5 kHz for greater low-end control, 11 kHz as a compromise, and a default 25 kHz camera mode; other ETC studio fixtures expose roughly 900 Hz–25 kHz. The tradeoff proves that higher carrier, dimming resolution, output, and efficiency cannot be assumed independent. ETC PWM modes · ETC studio range

Cinema validation practice

Camera acceptance uses the exact fixture firmware, brightness, color, camera, frame rate, shutter angle or exposure, and motion expected in the shot. Frequency may be tuned away from roll bars or beats. ARRI also offers a high-speed mode for extreme capture that removes dimming, while selectable frequencies retain dimming for conditions that permit it. Mixed fixtures must be checked together because unsynchronized modulation can beat. ARRI SkyPanel guidance

Laboratory drive practice

When modulation is unwanted, laboratory illumination prefers regulated constant current or constant optical power with ripple specified across a stated bandwidth—not an unqualified “flicker-free” label. Drivers are allowed to warm up, junction or fixture temperature is stabilized, and deliberate pulse experiments share a hardware trigger with the detector. One laboratory driver family, for example, specifies current noise from 1 Hz to 10 MHz and separately exposes DC, PWM, pulse, and arbitrary-waveform modes. Thorlabs laboratory LED-driver specifications

Laboratory optical practice

Electrical current and emitted light are measured as different quantities. A fast photodiode or high-speed photometer captures the temporal waveform; an integrating sphere and spectroradiometer measure total flux, spectrum, color, and efficacy while current, voltage, and temperature are recorded. Calibrated systems explicitly support DC and pulsed testing because neither predicts the other without measurement. Labsphere temporal measurement · Labsphere LED characterization

Flicker-minimization order

Prefer steady low-ripple current. If modulation is required, reduce modulation depth, avoid reaching zero, maximize duty while lowering On Current, suppress driver ripple and subharmonics, avoid beat frequencies between Channels or power stages, then move the remaining carrier above the relevant visibility and camera range. Never substitute frequency alone for waveform measurement.

Three-tier physical benchmark

The first hardware comparison must place the same LED load and mean-emission target under three controls: steady low-ripple DC, consumer-class 2–4 kHz PWM, and cinema-class 25 kHz PWM. Poisson remains the experimental fourth condition. Mean optical emission—not commanded mean current—must be matched before comparing temporal behavior.

Hardware acceptance

Measure emitted light—not commanded current—with a sufficiently fast photodiode and report the complete waveform, percent modulation, RMS modulation, peak and minimum, frequency spectrum, energy below 80 Hz, 2 kHz, and 12 kHz, PstLM, SVM, and an appropriate phantom-array measure. Repeat at worst-case dim levels, colors, supply and thermal states, with moving eyes and objects, sensitive viewers where appropriate, and every target camera configuration. Only that evidence can support an application-specific “imperceptible” claim.

06 · Testing decisions

Prove behavior at the public Interface.

Tests exercise the owned Poisson LED Lab Engine rather than reproducing its internals in a fake. Browser validation verifies the AudioWorklet adapter and rendered controls.

Arrival evidence

A long seeded run must reproduce exactly, approach the requested total rate, distribute counts near Λ/8, and preserve the sum of Channel counts as total Arrivals.

Signal evidence

Every Channel sample remains in [0,1), Aggregate White equals the exact sample mean, and every pre-gain audio sample equals Aggregate White minus Target Mean Current with no hidden transformation or state.

Gain evidence

The Monitor Gain slider must map equal travel to equal base-gain ratios from 0.01× through the exact target-dependent no-clipping bound. A 50% target permits 2× base; 25% or 75% permits 1.33×; 100% permits 1×. Effective PWM gain must always equal one quarter of effective Poisson gain at the same slider setting. Changing Target Mean Current must update and enforce the base bound immediately. Gain may alter only the browser GainNode; LED currents, event timing, worklet diagnostics, and the pre-gain Audio Oscilloscope must remain identical.

Continuity evidence

One render and arbitrarily partitioned renders must return identical Channel current, fused current, and audio samples for the same seed and settings.

Rate evidence

The maximum clamps to 48,000 total Arrivals/s and reports 6,000 expected Arrivals/s per Channel. Dense current must show lower RMS modulation than a tenfold slower field at the same target and fall time.

PWM evidence

At an 8,000/s total event budget, 25% target, and 100% On Current, every Channel must produce the same binary 1 kHz waveform, exactly 1,000 rising edges/s, 25% mean current, and 8,000 combined edges/s. At 50% On Current, its only current values must be 0% and 50%, derived duty must be 50%, and mean current must remain 25%. On Current may never fall below Target Mean Current. Both conditions must satisfy the same direct commanded-mean subtraction rule.

PWM timing evidence

At 1 kHz per Channel, a 25% target with 100% On Current must report 0.25 ms on and 0.75 ms silent. Reducing On Current to 50% must report 0.50 ms on and 0.50 ms silent while preserving the same 25% commanded mean.

Terminology evidence

The public engine Interface, control label, specification, and tests must use PWM On Current. The obsolete PWM Pulse Current field must not remain as a silent alias because two names would obscure which term is authoritative.

Full-DC evidence

At a 100% target, both conditions must emit exact unit current on all eight Channels and Aggregate White while their underlying event counts continue. Direct subtraction of the commanded unit mean must make every audio-monitor sample exactly zero without NaN, infinity, clipping logic, filter state, or a stopped clock.

Display evidence

The page exposes eighteen accessible meters in two labeled condition banks, two simultaneous accessible current scopes, one selected-source audio scope, one accessible monitor selector, one shared scope timebase, exact total/per-Channel language, Poisson report means, the active PWM LED Presentation with measured refresh, current diagnostics, and one explicit sound-monitor switch.

PWM presentation evidence

At measured 60 Hz or 120 Hz refresh, the resolved-quality ceiling must not exceed 15 Hz per Channel: 10 Hz must resolve, 12.5 Hz must use a bounded transition whose high and low currents both move toward the matched mean, and 15 Hz, 16 Hz, 137.5 Hz, and 1 kHz must remain integrated without report-window beating. Repeated 120 Hz observations must latch even when later frames alternate between 120 and 60 Hz cadence. Presentation changes must leave engine samples, scopes, event counts, audio, and metrics byte-for-byte unchanged.

Display-transfer evidence

The shared LED Display Transfer must map zero and full current to zero and full display code, bound invalid inputs, and map 50% current to a display level D where D^2.2 = 0.5. Both LED banks must call this same Adapter while retaining linear values in their accessible meters and diagnostics.

Scope evidence

A known PWM pulse train must retain zero and one plateaus after display reduction. When multiple excursions fall inside one column, the same Interface must retain their true minimum and maximum without normalization or invented zero crossings.

Browser evidence

Start must animate both banks and both current scopes. Monitor Source changes must preserve both current histories while changing selected metrics, the audio scope, and audible output; shared-control changes must not restart either condition. Sound disable must leave all LEDs and current scopes running, Stop must darken all eighteen meters, and the console and worklet must remain error-free at 48 kHz.

Solar-reference evidence

A future physical comparison must first match mean emitted optical power, then report temporal modulation from the same photodiode, bandwidth, aperture, and exposure for low-ripple DC, PWM, and Poisson. Spectral comparison must separately name the ASTM G173 direct-normal or global-tilt target and report the measured spectral residual; electrical mean current alone satisfies neither comparison.

08 · Further notes

The LED discs declare their limit; all three scopes remember and zoom.

A typical display cannot emit the worklet’s 48 kHz optical waveform. Poisson discs report complete report-interval means. PWM discs require four frames per resolved cycle, progressively converge toward the matched mean near a maximum 15 Hz per-Channel ceiling, and remain integrated above it rather than aliasing arbitrary report windows or dropped frames. Both banks apply the same inverse display transfer so equal current targets produce comparable screen luminance. The simultaneous fixed-scale Poisson and PWM Current Oscilloscopes plus the selected-source Audio Oscilloscope remain authoritative for exact DC and AC evidence. Sunlight supplies the conceptual reference: spectrally broadband and atmosphere-dependent, yet effectively steady in macroscopic time. A future physical build must benchmark steady DC, consumer-class PWM, cinema-class PWM, and Poisson on the same optical load with photodiode, thermal, spectral, and camera evidence before any claim about solar similarity or imperceptible modulation is accepted.
