01 · Problem statement

The optical hypothesis needs its own clean bench.

The existing prototype begins with an acoustic waveform and derives light from it. That makes the LED result inseparable from decisions made for sound. This experiment instead needs to reveal what a bounded, positive, steady Poisson current field does before any acoustic interpretation exists.

Current lacks authority

An audio-first path allows an acoustic decision to change the LED waveform. The optical experiment cannot then distinguish Poisson behavior from source-model behavior.

Rate is easily misread

Speed must continue to mean one total lamp rate. At the maximum, 48,000 Arrivals/s total becomes an expected 6,000 Arrivals/s per Channel—not 48 kHz on every Channel.

A monitor can hide convergence

Automatic level matching would keep high-density modulation loud even as the current becomes steadier. The monitor must preserve the current’s changing AC magnitude.

02 · Solution

Generate positive current, then listen to what remains after DC.

One homogeneous Poisson clock emits exact Arrivals. Uniform marking splits them into eight independent Channel streams. Each Arrival injects the same nonnegative current response; higher density reduces charge per Arrival so selected mean current stays comparable. Aggregate White is the normalized fused monitor.

03 · User stories

The lab should make the hypothesis falsifiable.

Every control and measurement exists to reveal current behavior, not to decorate it.

  1. As an experimenter, I want to sweep one logarithmic total rate from 1 through 48 kHz, so that sparse flashes and dense convergence occupy one continuous control.
  2. As an experimenter, I want every Arrival assigned uniformly to exactly one of eight Channels, so that Channel count does not multiply the lamp’s selected event rate.
  3. As an experimenter, I want a ninth white monitor formed from all eight currents, so that I can inspect the complete field without inventing a ninth stream.
  4. As an experimenter, I want average current held approximately constant as Speed changes, so that increasing brightness or saturation cannot masquerade as convergence.
  5. As an experimenter, I want to change current fall time, so that I can study how response bandwidth changes overlap and fluctuation.
  6. As an experimenter, I want to hear only the AC portion of fused current, so that audible modulation corresponds directly to optical modulation.
  7. As an experimenter, I want fixed monitor gain, so that genuinely diminishing modulation is allowed to become quiet.
  8. As an experimenter, I want each virtual LED to show the arithmetic mean of every current sample in its display interval, so that no Arrival is silently discarded.
  9. As an experimenter, I want a one-second min/max-preserving scope, so that sub-frame current structure remains visible even though the DOM LEDs update at monitor refresh rate.
  10. As an experimenter, I want mean, RMS modulation, peak, limiter proximity, and observed rate together, so that I can distinguish convergence from clipping or missed events.
  11. As an experimenter, I want Stop to force zero sound and zero current immediately, so that stopped state is unambiguous.
  12. As a future hardware designer, I want the browser’s display-refresh limitation stated plainly, so that this page is not mistaken for a physical flicker-perception test.
04 · Implementation decisions

One deep Module owns the complete signal contract.

The Poisson LED Lab Engine Interface is the sole behavioral seam. The worklet adapts rendered blocks to audio and diagnostic messages; the page adapts those messages to pixels and controls.

Exact timing

Inter-arrival gaps are exponentially distributed. Multiple Arrivals may occur inside one 48 kHz sample interval and each contributes independently; sample coincidence never merges event identity or energy.

Uniform routing

Every Arrival receives one uniform Channel mark. Poisson splitting gives eight independent streams with expected rate Λ/8 and preserves Λ as the total lamp rate.

Positive response

An Arrival injects a positive exponential current response with selectable fall time τ. There are no spectral, spatial, acoustic, or event-morphology marks.

Mean-held charge

Event charge varies inversely with expected Channel rate and response duration. At high overlap, the smooth limiter’s expected output approaches Target Mean Current. Sparse bounded pulses cannot always reach that target and must report their actual mean honestly.

Smooth limit

Raw positive shot current maps through u/(1+u). The current remains continuous in value, nonnegative, and below 100%, without a hard clipping plateau.

White fusion

Aggregate White equals the arithmetic mean of the eight instantaneous Channel currents. It has no Arrivals, response state, or independent gain of its own.

Direct sound monitor

A standard first-order 2 Hz DC blocker converts fused current to a signed AC signal. The only later operation is the explicit user-controlled output level. The signal is copied equally to both speakers.

Frame integration

Every worklet sample contributes to the arithmetic mean displayed by each DOM LED. Measurements are diagnostic views, not selectable rendering modes. The scope uses per-pixel minima and maxima to retain excursions.

Isolation

This standalone file and its Modules do not call, configure, or revise the existing generated-audio path. The two experiments share only durable domain definitions for Arrival, Channel, Speed, and Aggregate White.

05 · Testing decisions

Prove behavior at the public Interface.

Tests exercise the owned Poisson LED Lab Engine rather than reproducing its internals in a fake. Browser validation verifies the AudioWorklet adapter and rendered controls.

Arrival evidence

A long seeded run must reproduce exactly, approach the requested total rate, distribute counts near Λ/8, and preserve the sum of Channel counts as total Arrivals.

Signal evidence

Every Channel sample remains in [0,1), Aggregate White equals the exact sample mean, and the audio output satisfies the declared DC-block recurrence with no hidden transformation.

Continuity evidence

One render and arbitrarily partitioned renders must return identical Channel current, fused current, and audio samples for the same seed and settings.

Rate evidence

The maximum clamps to 48,000 total Arrivals/s and reports 6,000 expected Arrivals/s per Channel. Dense current must show lower RMS modulation than a tenfold slower field at the same target and fall time.

Display evidence

The page exposes nine accessible meters, exact total/per-Channel language, the fixed frame-mean rule, the current diagnostics, and one explicit sound-monitor switch.

Browser evidence

Start must produce changing current, rate changes must not restart the page, sound disable must leave LEDs running, Stop must darken all meters, and the console and worklet must remain error-free at 48 kHz.

06 · Out of scope

This is a stochastic-current instrument, not yet a driver.

The page measures a mathematical and perceptual direction before circuit topology is selected.

Excluded source behavior

  • Recorded or generated environmental sound
  • Per-event amplitude or response-family marks
  • Distance, propagation, stereo position, material, or wavelength-driven timbre
  • Shared Channel coupling or a ninth event stream

Excluded optical claims

  • Calibrated luminance, radiometry, or chromaticity
  • Retinal persistence or human flicker thresholds
  • Proof of 48 kHz optical perception on a 60 Hz display
  • Thermal behavior or LED efficiency

Deferred hardware

  • DAC resolution and reconstruction filtering
  • Constant-current topology and feedback bandwidth
  • MCU event scheduling and interrupt load
  • PCB, power, thermal, and safety requirements
07 · Further notes

The virtual LEDs integrate; the scope remembers.

A typical display cannot emit the worklet’s 48 kHz optical waveform. Its LED circles therefore report one honest exposure-like frame mean. The adjacent current scope and statistics carry the evidence lost to refresh-rate integration. A future physical build must repeat the experiment with a measured current driver and photodiode before any claim about imperceptible modulation is accepted.
